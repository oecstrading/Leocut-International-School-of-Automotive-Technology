LEOCUT / 100-STUDENT MASTERPLAN

Design for 100. Launch with funded demand.

This expanded plan sizes Leocut for 100 new full-programme learners annually: 60 in Automotive Service and Diagnostics and 40 in Collision Repair and Refinishing. The recommended first operating year enrols 80, rising to 100 from Year 2. Capacity is installed for 100 at opening; a full first intake is possible only when funding, staff and placements are secured. Eighty optional short-course enrolments at maturity are additional course registrations, not 80 additional guaranteed unique people.

The earlier EC$1.30m / 6,000 sq ft proposal served 36–48 long-course learners. This plan replaces those capacity assumptions for the 100-student option; it is a new scope, not a simple multiplication of the old budget. Costs remain pre-feasibility allowances pending Grenada quotations, site selection and qualification mapping.

Outcomes at maturity: 100 enrolled → target 90 completions → target 72 progressing into employment, apprenticeship or self-employment within six months. These are targets, not promised jobs. With 90% completion, 112 entrants would be required to graduate 100; that would exceed this design and require rescheduling, staff and budget changes.

Practices to adopt from the start

Publish a task-based curriculum; give every learner a competence portfolio; use written employer training agreements; maintain an employer advisory board; keep instructor technical skills current; isolate hazardous processes from classrooms; and audit equipment against actual assessment tasks. Do not measure quality by the number of vehicles in the yard.

The new ASE automobile standards specify 540, 840 and 1,200 classroom/shop hours for successive programme levels [5]. Leocut proposes 960 campus hours plus 240 placement hours and must not advertise this as a 1,200-hour ASE Master programme. Hours alone never establish accreditation. Use a documented gap analysis before any accreditation claim.

Site selection and engineering brief

Allow approximately 20,000 sq ft for rear/side vehicle circulation, parking, deliveries and secured training vehicles; a one-acre site then leaves about 11,560 sq ft for setbacks, drainage, landscape and protected pedestrian access. This is a land-use test, not a planning entitlement. Any larger code-required setbacks may require 1.5 acres. Select a bus-accessible site with drainage, three-phase supply where required, sound slabs and clearance suitable for the chosen lifts.

Provide separate public and vehicle entrances, controlled keys, accessible routes and facilities, clean classroom air, local extraction at source, acoustic separation, daylight without glare, hurricane-resistant construction and secured roof-mounted equipment. A Grenada architect, structural/MEP engineer and fire/environmental advisers must validate zoning, occupancy, lift anchorage, ventilation, electrical capacity, drainage, exits and waste storage.

Do not fix booth location solely from this diagram: verify prevailing winds, exhaust discharge and nearby air intakes. Separate paint processes from welding and ignition sources. External hazardous stores and damaged-EV exclusion arrangements require an engineered location and emergency-service agreement. Do not accept damaged high-voltage batteries for storage at opening.

Practical weekly pattern

Operate the facility across 45 scheduled teaching hours per week, with staggered learner timetables of 30 contact hours. An indicative opening span is 07:30–18:00, including breaks and cleaning; instructors work contracted shifts rather than the full span. A two-week campus cycle gives each cohort 22.5 hours combined theory plus 37.5 hours of practical learning per learner. The practical component is repeated for its two groups of ten.

At full campus attendance, mechanical practice requires 60 × 18.75 = 1,125 learner-hours/week versus 30 simultaneous places × 45 = 1,350 available (83.3% use). Collision practice requires 40 × 18.75 = 750 against 20 × 45 = 900 (83.3%). Theory requires 56.25 cohort-room hours/week across four teaching rooms. These are capacity checks; the room-by-room and instructor-by-instructor timetable remains a pre-launch approval item.

Placement — 240 hours per learner

Use a signed learner/employer/Leocut agreement, a named qualified mentor, approved task list, attendance records, weekly learner log and at least two assessor contacts. Agree insurance, safe transport, emergency procedures, confidentiality, harassment reporting and any stipend before placement. Employers must provide relevant competence evidence, not just cleaning tasks. A failed or unsafe placement requires reassignment and completion of missing hours.

Admission and inclusion

Normally age 18+, with a practical aptitude interview, basic literacy/numeracy diagnostic and occupational health review appropriate to workshop risks. Do not require a driving licence for admission; road tests are performed only by authorised licensed staff. Offer a separate pre-entry bridging course of up to 60 hours, outside the 1,200 hours and separately funded if needed. Recognise prior learning only through approved evidence and assessor processes. Provide accessible learning materials, adjustments, suitable PPE and confidential learner support; select on demonstrated aptitude and readiness.

Assessment and completion rules

Proposed assessment profile: 60% observed practical performance, 20% knowledge checks, 10% portfolio/job records and 10% capstone. Percentage scores track progress; they do not override competency requirements. Every mandatory task and all critical safety behaviours must be competent. Use an internal 70% knowledge threshold, reassessment plans and an appeal procedure, subject to GNTA rules. Require at least 90% attendance plus make-up of all missing required contact hours and competence evidence; attendance alone never earns an award.

Moderate a sample of assessments each term, double-mark capstones, maintain assessor independence and track results by cohort, sex, parish and funding source. Budget instructor development and adopt at least 20 hours of relevant technical updating annually as a Leocut policy benchmark [5].

Safety controls that change the design

HSE guidance is an international good-practice reference, not Grenada law. Its spray-painting advice supports a contained extracted booth, controlled breathing-air provision, tested clearance time and health surveillance for isocyanate exposure [7]. Leocut must not substitute an ordinary disposable mask for a suitable supplied-air system when spraying isocyanate products. Engineer and commission all controls before practical spraying.

For EV work, obtain vehicle-specific information, restrict access and verify competence for each task. HSE distinguishes awareness from repair and high-voltage work [8]. At launch use de-energised demonstrators and awareness exercises; prohibit live high-voltage instruction and battery-pack opening. Specialist intervention requires a separately approved programme, suitable facilities and qualified personnel.

Financial model rules and limitations

EC$ nominal; five full operating years after pre-opening. Base enrolment 80/100/100/100/100; the 80 opening intake is 40 mechanical + 40 collision. Capacity capped at 100; short courses 60/80/80/80/80, workshop sales 225k/300k/300k/300k/300k. Controls scale long-course volumes only; ancillary revenue has its own multiplier. All staffing/fixed costs are retained even in downside.

Tuition, short-course fees and per-learner variable costs rise 3% each year; workshop sales rise 3% after applying the listed volumes; fixed costs rise 4%. Equipment/design assets EC$2.42m depreciate over 10 years. EC$480k pre-opening and contingency are conservatively expensed before operations. Maintenance capex EC$36k/year grows 3%, depreciated over five years from acquisition. Actual contingency capitalisation and asset lives need an accountant’s schedule.

Loan EC$900k, monthly interest-only during the two-year principal grace; then equal monthly instalments over remaining term. Grant is excluded from revenue and treated as a capital funding reserve. Opening cash = EC$900k less grant shortfall. Annual receivables = public share of tuition × collection days/365; other sales collected in-year. No opening receivables, inventories, trade payables, bad debts or additional borrowing assumed. Cash changes reflect receivable movements, debt, capex and illustrative tax.

Illustrative 28% tax on positive pre-tax profit; no loss carryforward, deferred tax or grant amortisation. This is an unconfirmed planning rate, not a statement of the school’s tax liability. Revenues and costs are net of recoverable VAT; irrecoverable taxes/import charges belong in actual quotes. DSCR = (EBITDA − tax − maintenance capex) / debt service. A negative balance is an unfunded shortfall. No dividend, terminal value or equity IRR is asserted.

Minimum liquidity test: EC$300k. Proposed mature DSCR covenant ≥1.25×; request a specifically documented ramp-up exception if needed. Before loan submission replace the annual DSO approximation with monthly tuition instalments, actual public payment terms and commissioning drawdowns.
